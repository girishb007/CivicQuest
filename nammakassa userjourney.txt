4. The map, clusters, and hotspots
4.1 Visual layer — CONFIRMED from screenshots you provided
Ward choropleth: every ward polygon filled red, opacity scales with report count in that ward (dense wards like Malleshwaram/Mahalakshmi Layout are near-solid red; empty wards are nearly white).
Pin colors map to severity: yellow (Minor) → orange (Moderate) → red (Severe) → dark maroon (Critical), plus green for Resolved.
Numbered dark circles (e.g. 26, 11, 9, 7, 5, 4) — these are clusters: multiple reports collapsed into one marker at the current zoom level, count shown on the marker.
Larger dark maroon circles with a soft halo and no number — these are hotspots: repeated dumping concentrated in one small area, visually emphasized so chronic sites read louder than isolated litter.

4.3 Filing a new report — CONFIRMED: QR-only on desktop, no desktop form exists

Clicking "Scan QR to Report" opens a modal containing a QR code generated via api.qrserver.com/v1/create-qr-code/ and a close button. That's it — there is no desktop complaint form at all, not even hidden behind another click. Filing only happens on a phone after scanning the QR (presumably landing on a mobile capture view). This confirms my earlier inference and forecloses the question — build the same split: map/browse/leaderboard fully on desktop, capture flow mobile-only.

What it is: a self-registered directory of cleanup groups (RWAs, ploggers, apartment associations) operating in Bengaluru. Example entries seen in the directory: Team socialspotlight, NammaKasa Community, Ploggers Army.

Buttons: + Register your community (entry point) → form → Cancel / Submit.

How you get this dataset: you don't pull it from anywhere — you build the same self-registration flow. There is no government or open dataset of RWAs/volunteer groups (I checked in the earlier pass — nothing exists nationally or for Bengaluru). NammaKasa's own approach is the answer: let groups register themselves, collect a private name+phone for verification, and moderate manually before listing publicly. That "Not shown publicly" field is their anti-spam gate — a human presumably reviews new submissions using that contact before they go live.

7.2 What actually happens when you tap the MLA or MP face — CORRECTING my earlier guess

I previously assumed this opens a contact panel (phone/email/X handle). That was wrong — I tested it, and here's what's actually there:

Tapping Dr. C. N. Ashwathnarayan's card opens an accountability analytics panel, not a contact card:

Field shown	Value
Constituency	Malleshwaram
Party	BJP
Active (unresolved) reports across the constituency	92
Total reports (all-time)	147
Average days unresolved	92
Wards in this constituency	10
Worst wards, ranked	Malleshwaram, Subramanyanagara, Sadashiva Nagara, Rajamahal, Mathikere (each with its own count)

No phone number, email, or X/Twitter handle appeared in this panel. Tapping the MP's card opens the same shape of panel (role, party, constituency rollup) — same absence of direct contact info.

Why this matters for your clone: this is a much better feature than a contact card. It's not "here's how to email your MLA" — it's "here's the aggregate evidence against your MLA's entire constituency, ranked by which of their wards is worst." That's the actual accountability mechanism, and it's more damning and harder to dismiss than a single dump photo. Build this panel, not a rolodex entry. The footer text "Tap any card for contact options" may be leftover/aspirational copy that doesn't match current behavior, or contact options may appear on a different card in the chain (BSWML or the officer nodes) that this pass didn't click — worth a follow-up test specifically on those.
